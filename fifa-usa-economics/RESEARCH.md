# THE ECONOMICS OF FIFA IN AMERICA
### Greenback — Episode 2 research dossier (deep research, as of 30 September 2026)

**Scope.** How FIFA makes money in the United States, who pays for it, and what the 2026 World Cup (11 June – 19 July 2026, USA/Canada/Mexico) actually delivered. Covers FIFA's own accounts, the 2025 Club World Cup dress rehearsal, the 11 US host cities, the fans' ledger, the TV market, the Trump–Infantino relationship, the tax position, and the 30-year US–FIFA history from the 1994 World Cup to the 2015 Justice Department indictments.

**Method.** Five parallel research passes (roughly 200 web searches and 200 page fetches in total) plus direct cross-checks of every headline number. Every figure carries a source and date. FIFA's audited 2026 numbers do not exist yet (the FIFA Annual Report 2026 is due around March 2027), so anything about 2026 revenue is a FIFA forecast or statement, and is labelled as such.

**Confidence key.** ✅ primary source or two independent secondary sources · ⚠️ single secondary source or snippet only · ❓ UNCONFIRMED (could not be verified; do not put on screen without checking).

## 0. The story in one page

1. **FIFA bet its biggest cycle ever on America.** The 2023–26 cycle was budgeted at $11B, revised to $13B, and Infantino told member associations on 18 July 2026 it will "top $15 billion". The 2026 World Cup alone was forecast at $8.9B (2025) then ~$11B (April 2026). ✅
2. **The tournament worked as a product.** 6,810,966 spectators over 104 matches, average 65,490, 99.7% of seats filled, a US TV final audience of 66.4M across Fox and Telemundo/Peacock, $1.19B in US ad sales, Adidas shirt sales four times Qatar. ✅
3. **It worked because FIFA changed the deal.** For the first time FIFA ran the World Cup itself through a US subsidiary, kept 100% of ticket, hospitality, sponsorship and media revenue, took a 15% fee from both sides of every resale, priced tickets dynamically ($60 to $10,990 face value; $2.3M listings on its own platform), and demanded sales-tax exemptions, "clean" stadiums and free offices from every host. ✅
4. **The hosts paid.** Economists put each US host city's cost at $100M–200M; identified public commitments run to about $245–307M in New York/New Jersey and about $230M in Kansas City; Washington ~$120M; Miami ~$58M. Congress added $625M for security, $250M for counter-drone systems and $100M for transit. FIFA's legacy promise to each city was $1M, unpaid as of August 2026. ✅
5. **The promised boom mostly did not show up.** Hotel occupancy was below the no-tournament counterfactual in 7 of 11 US markets (HVS); incremental room revenue was $680M against a $900M forecast; overseas arrivals to the US reportedly fell 7% year on year in July 2026; 80% of hoteliers reported bookings below forecast before kickoff. Rates rose; rooms did not fill. ✅
6. **Politics was part of the business model.** A White House task force, FIFA's office in Trump Tower, the inaugural "FIFA Peace Prize" for Trump at the draw, visa fast-tracks for ticket holders while fans from banned countries (Iran, Haiti, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire) stayed home, and a Justice Department that has now abandoned the bribery theory behind its own 2015 FIFA prosecutions. ✅/⚠️
7. **The paradox.** The US is the one market where FIFA is treated as a victim of corruption, a tax-exempt non-profit, and a guest of honour at the White House, all at once. The 1994 World Cup left a $50M surplus to a US charity. The 2026 World Cup leaves billions in Zurich and the bills in city halls.

## 2. The dress rehearsal: Club World Cup 2025 (USA, 14 June – 13 July 2025)

- **$1B prize pool** = $525M participation + $475M performance. Chelsea (winner) $114.6M; PSG $107M; Real Madrid $82.5M; Fluminense $60.8M; Man City $51.7M; Inter Miami $21.1M; LAFC $10.6M; Seattle $9.6M; Auckland City $4.6M (about eight times its annual revenue). ✅ NBC Sports 13 Jul 2025: https://www.nbcsports.com/soccer/news/2025-fifa-club-world-cup-prize-money-list-details-how-much-will-each-club-make ; Football Benchmark 17 Jul 2025.
- **Where the money came from:** DAZN global rights **$1B**, free-to-air (Telegraph, 4 Dec 2024) → FIFA awards Saudi Arabia the 2034 World Cup unopposed (11 Dec 2024) → Saudi PIF's SURJ Sports Investment takes a minority stake in DAZN, press-reported at ~$1B for ~10% (17 Feb 2025) ✅ DAZN release: https://dazngroup.com/press-room/surj-sports-investment-announces-investment-in-dazn/ ; PIF also a tournament sponsor. Sponsorship "sold out" 13 Jul 2025 (Hisense, AB InBev, Bank of America, Coca-Cola, Visa, Qatar Airways, Aramco, Adidas, Hyundai/Kia, Lenovo, PIF, Airbnb, Home Depot, Panini, Jeep).
- **Audited outcome:** revenue **$2.13B** = broadcast ~$1.0B + sponsorship $669M + ticketing $411M + licensing $5M (FIFA Annual Report 2025 via IWF, 23 Mar 2026). Infantino on 13 Jul 2025: "almost USD 2.1 billion… USD 33 million per match". Net result never disclosed ❓.
- **Attendance:** 2,491,462 over 63 matches, avg 39,547; ~1.67M seats unsold; only 25 of 48 group games in stadiums more than half full; Ulsan v Mamelodi Sundowns in Orlando: 3,412 official, under 1,000 at kick-off per ESPN. ✅ Wikipedia; Managing Sport and Leisure (2025); ESPN.
- **Dynamic pricing collapse:** Chelsea v Fluminense semi-final at MetLife cut from $473.90 to **$13.40**; quarter-finals $11.15; final cheapest ticket $312.20 → $201.75. Miami Dade College students: one $20 ticket plus four free for the opener. ✅ AP/NBC New York 6 Jul 2025; Forbes 8 Jul 2025; Goal 12 Jul 2025.
- Infantino, Sep 2025: "I didn't know about [dynamic pricing], but it means that the prices can go up or can go down… the important element is to fill stadiums." ✅ ESPN.
- **Players:** FIFPRO Europe + European Leagues legal claim (13 Jun 2024) and European Commission complaint under Art. 102 (14 Oct 2024). Heat and lightning: six matches suspended in the first 50, Chelsea v Benfica delayed ~2 hours; FIFPRO called it a "wake-up call" (1 Jul 2025); Klopp: "worst idea ever implemented in football". ✅
- **US TV:** TNT averaged 360k; final 1.3M on TNT + 1.4M on Univision. ✅ FOS 27 Jun 2025.
- **Lesson FIFA drew:** keep dynamic pricing, price higher from the start. Opening 2026 range $60–$6,730 announced 3 Sep 2025; FIFA COO Heimo Schirgi: "optimizing the revenue, but also optimizing attendance… always a balance". ✅ NBC News.

## 3. The host cities: who paid

### 3.1 The terms (host city agreements, signed with FWC2026 US, Inc.)
- Cities bear "all taxes, duties and levies" and "overall safety and security"; get **no share of tickets, concessions, merchandise or parking**; local sponsorship sales restricted (NY/NJ host committee limited to 20 local deals, fewer than 10 sold); FIFA's stadium rent redacted; free "state-of-the-art" office space required. Contrast 1994, when cities shared game-day revenue and US Soccer paid for security. ✅ ProPublica/Texas Tribune, 29 Apr 2026: https://www.propublica.org/article/world-cup-2026-host-cities-revenue-houston ; ITEP 5 Dec 2025: https://itep.org/fifa-2026-world-cup-tickets-sales-tax-exemption/ ; Bergen Record 19 May 2026.
- "Clean" stadiums: "New York New Jersey Stadium", "Dallas Stadium", "Los Angeles Stadium" etc. ✅
- Ticket sales-tax exemptions demanded as a hosting condition and granted by every host state; forgone: Georgia up to $25M, Missouri ~$11M (SB 652), Florida ~$7.4M; Kansas City area $15.7M ⚠️. ✅ ITEP.
- FIFA's legacy payment: **$1M per city** (same as the Club World Cup), promised verbally, **unpaid as of 4 Aug 2026**. ⚠️ Fox 9 / The Athletic, 4 Aug 2026: https://www.fox9.com/sports/fifa-world-cup-us-cities-egacy-contribution

**Claims:** BCG for the bid (Feb 2018): $5B+ for North America, $160–620M per city. ## 4. The fans' ledger

### 4.1 Prices
- Opening range announced 3 Sep 2025: **$60 to $6,730** (final, Category 1), first-ever World Cup dynamic pricing. ✅ NBC News.
- Top face value rose repeatedly: $6,730 (Sep 2025) → **$8,680** (phase 3, Dec 2025; cheapest group ticket then $140) → **$10,990** at the April 2026 sales reopening. ✅ ESPN 24 Mar 2026; Al Jazeera 12 Dec 2025; Gulf News Apr 2026. (Fortune's "$7,875 Category 1 final" on 19 Jun 2026 is a third figure; label whichever you use with its date.)
- Group-stage Category 1 up to **$575** vs $220 in Qatar; some group tickets over $1,000. 1994 range: $25–475. The 2018 United bid book had promised $21 group tickets. ✅ Fortune 21 Jul 2026; Al Jazeera 12 Dec 2025.
- FIFA's own last-minute final listings: **$7,380–$32,970**; get-in fell from $12,200 (22 Jun) to $7,149 (10 Jul) after FIFA released 1,500 extra seats at $7,380; 1,366 final seats still unsold on 10 July. ✅ Newsweek 10 Jul 2026: https://www.newsweek.com/world-cup-final-and-semi-finals-ticket-prices-plunge-12182359
- Resale on FIFA's platform: four final tickets listed at **$2,299,998.85 each** (block 124, row 45); a last-row upper-deck seat at $138,000; SeatGeek average >$11,000, about 8% above the 2024 Super Bowl. FIFA takes **15% from the buyer and 15% from the seller**; Mexico forced face-value-only resale, the US and Canada did not. ✅ Al Jazeera 18 Jul 2026: https://www.aljazeera.com/sports/2026/7/18/world-cup-final-tickets-near-2-3m-mark-on-fifas-resale-platform ; AP/WISN Apr 2026.
- Comparators: Qatar 2022 cheapest group ticket $69, final $604–1,607; Russia 2018 final $455–1,100. ✅ ESPN 2022.

### 4.2 Backlash and FIFA's response

### 4.4 Visas, the travel ban, ICE

Four qualified teams affected: **Iran, Haiti** (full ban), **Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal** (partial). ✅ CBS Sports 5 Jun 2025; Forum Together 19 Mar 2026.
- **FIFA PASS** priority visa appointments for ticket holders, announced 17 Nov 2025 at the White House (Trump, Rubio, Noem, Infantino); 400+ then 600+ extra consular officers. ✅ https://inside.fifa.com/organisation/media-releases/world-cup-2026-ticket-holders-prioritised-visa-appointments-united-states ; State Dept.
- $250 "visa integrity fee" (B1/B2 to ~$435) ⚠️ timing disputed; visa bonds $5,000–15,000 for ~50 countries, waived for FIFA PASS enrollees from Algeria, Cape Verde, Côte d'Ivoire, Senegal, Tunisia. ✅ Time 14 May 2026.
- Senegal sent no official delegation for the first time; supporters' club presidents refused visas; 400 tickets per match given to the diaspora. ✅ France 24, 15 Jun 2026. Iran's squad based in Mexico and admitted to the US the day before its LA matches; FIFA withdrew Iran's fan allocation; Iran v New Zealand at SoFi drew 70,000+. ✅ NPR 24 Jun 2026; Al Jazeera 16 Jun 2026. Iraq's Aymen Hussein held ~7 hours at O'Hare. ⚠️
- DHS Secretary Mullin, 13 May 2026: "ICE always does immigration enforcement… but we're not there for solely that purpose", contradicting Rubio's assurance to Miami. ✅ Yahoo; NPR 16 May 2026. Club World Cup precedent: an asylum seeker detained at MetLife on 13 Jul 2025, held 11 weeks (HRW 3 Dec 2025). Massachusetts habeas filings +21% during the tournament (WGBH 7 Jul 2026). No confirmed in-stadium fan detention during the World Cup. ✅/❓
- Human rights: Dignity 2026 coalition (HRW, AFL-CIO, NAACP, Athlete Ally and others) demands 3 Dec 2025; Amnesty "Humanity Must Win" 30 Mar 2026; ~65,000 unpaid, self-funded volunteers ⚠️.
- Heat: mandatory 3-minute breaks at 22' each half in all 104 matches; FIFPRO wanted WBGT thresholds; six cities rated "extremely high risk"; Houston Fan Festival 13 June: 110 treated, 4 hospitalised; Miami's first four matches: 37 heat cases, 5 hospitalised. ✅ E&E News 2 Jul 2026; WLRN 2 Jul 2026.

## 7. FIFA's US legal and tax position
- **Operating vehicle:** FWC2026 US, Inc., Delaware non-stock corporation, IRS-recognised **501(c)(4)** (EIN 87-2612719, Coral Gables), the "FIFA Ticket Seller" for US matches and hospitality. Form 990 FY2024: revenue $317,866, expenses $2.94M, net assets −$5.51M. Whether tournament revenue was booked here or in Zurich ❓. ✅ ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer: https://projects.propublica.org/nonprofits/organizations/872612719
- Offices: Coral Gables, 75,000 sq ft, ~350 staff, legal/audit/compliance moved from Zurich (Bisnow 27 Aug 2024); Trump Tower, New York (July 2025), rent undisclosed. ✅
- **Teams' tax:** after months of lobbying, Treasury agreed (late April 2026) that the 48 associations could seek non-profit exemption on team earnings; players and staff remain taxable. ✅ https://kpmg.com/us/en/taxnewsflash/news/2026/06/kpmg-article-fifa-2026-world-cup-tax-exemption.html
- **State/local:** sales-tax exemption on tickets required as a hosting condition; all host states complied (Missouri SB 652, June 2022; Georgia; Florida etc.). ✅ ITEP.
- "FIFA has enjoyed tax-exempt status in the US since 1994" ❓ (Global Treasurer claim, unverified).

### 7.1 The Justice Department and FIFA: from prosecutor to protector
- 27 May 2015: 47-count EDNY indictment of 14 (9 FIFA officials, 5 executives), "well over $150 million" in bribes; AG Loretta Lynch. 3 Dec 2015: 16 more. Tally: 27 individual guilty pleas, 4 corporate pleas; Napout and Marin convicted at trial (Dec 2017). ✅ DOJ: https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/nine-fifa-officials-and-five-corporate-executives-indicted-racketeering-conspiracy-and
- Blatter resigned 2 Jun 2015, four days after re-election; banned Dec 2015; acquitted in Switzerland (Jul 2022, upheld 25 Mar 2025). Infantino elected 26 Feb 2016 (115 of 207). ✅

- **FIFA as victim:** 25 Aug 2021, DOJ granted remission of up to **$201M** to FIFA, Concacaf and CONMEBOL as victims, via a World Football Remission Fund under the FIFA Foundation ($32.3M initial, ~$92M later). ✅ https://inside.fifa.com/organisation/media-releases/us-department-of-justice-awards-fifa-foundation-usd-201-million-compensation

## 11. Proposed episode shape: "HOW FIFA SOLD AMERICA THE WORLD CUP" (~17 min)

**Cold open · "The Peace Prize" (0:00–1:30).** Kennedy Center, 5 December 2025. The World Cup draw. Before a ball is drawn, Gianni Infantino hands Donald Trump a trophy that did not exist a month earlier: the first FIFA Peace Prize. "One of the great honors of my life." Cut to the price tag: seven months later, four tickets to the final sit on FIFA's own website at $2,299,998.85 each. FIFA takes 15% from whoever sells and 15% from whoever buys. Cut to a city hall: New York's comptroller says the city will spend $70M to earn at most $55M. Question: *FIFA said this World Cup would make $15 billion. Who paid for it?* Title.

1. **The template (1994).** 3.59M fans, $50M surplus to charity, cities shared the gate, US Soccer paid the security. The deal FIFA used to accept.
2. **The prosecutor becomes the host (2015–2018).** The DOJ indictments, Blatter's fall, Infantino's rise, the 2018 vote and Trump's tweet, the bid book's $11B promise, and the $201M the DOJ handed FIFA as a "victim".
3. **The new deal (the host city agreement).** FIFA runs it itself through a Delaware non-profit; 100% of tickets, hospitality, sponsorship, media; clean stadiums; tax exemptions; free offices; $1M legacy. Matheson: "you can squeeze all the money out of them you can."
4. **The rehearsal (Club World Cup 2025).** $1B prize pool bought with Saudi-backed DAZN money; $13.40 semi-final tickets; 3,412 in Orlando; Trump on the stage. Lesson: price high, and hold.
5. **The price of a seat (2026).** $60 to $10,990, dynamic pricing, the $2.3M listing, the $60 tier for "hundreds" per match, FSE's "legal black market", the EC complaint. And the sell-through: 99.7%. It worked.
6. **The bill (host cities).** NY/NJ $245–307M; KC $230M; the $625M federal cheque that arrived late; fan fests cancelled; the $150 train ticket. HVS: hotels emptier than a normal summer in 7 of 11 cities; overseas arrivals down 7%.
7. **Who couldn't come.** Travel ban: Iran, Haiti, Senegal, Côte d'Ivoire fans; FIFA PASS for those with tickets; ICE at the gates. A record crowd of the "happy few".
8. **Who won.** FIFA (~$11B tournament, $15B cycle, $14B next cycle); Fox and Telemundo ($1.19B ads on ~$485M of rights); Adidas (4× shirts); the NFL owners (rent, and a summer off); Spain ($50M). The cities got a bill and a plaque. Closing line: *In 1994 America hosted a World Cup and kept the change. In 2026 it hosted the same tournament and paid for the privilege. FIFA didn't come to America to grow the game. It came because America was the only place big enough to sell it.*

**Sibling episode note.** Episode 1 argued F1 got rich by becoming a closed franchise. This one argues FIFA got rich by turning host countries into franchisees: they pay to operate; the franchisor keeps the receipts.
