How the check works

1. File evidence. Many generators write into the image file itself. Stable Diffusion and ComfyUI save the prompt, and ChatGPT, Adobe Firefly, Google and others add Content Credentials (C2PA) or the IPTC tag "trained algorithmic media". If we find these, the answer is close to certain. A camera make and model in the file points the other way.

2. AI detector model. A SwinV2 image classifier (Apache 2.0) trained to tell AI images from real photos runs in your browser and gives a probability.

Be careful with the result. No detector is perfect. Screenshots, heavy compression, filters and images saved from social media (which removes metadata) all make detection harder. Treat the model's score as a hint, not proof.

Questions

Can it detect ChatGPT and Midjourney images?

Often, yes. Original downloads from ChatGPT, DALL-E, Firefly and many other tools carry Content Credentials or other markers in the file. If the image was screenshotted or re-uploaded to social media, those markers are usually gone and only the model's estimate is left.

Is my image uploaded?

No. Both checks run in your browser. The detector model is downloaded once and then cached.

Why does a real photo sometimes score as AI?

Heavy editing, beauty filters, HDR and upscaling can look "AI-like" to a model. That is why we show the file evidence separately and never claim 100% certainty.
