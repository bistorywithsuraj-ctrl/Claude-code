How the estimate works

Creators are paid per 1,000 views, a number YouTube calls RPM (revenue per mille). RPM depends mostly on two things: what your videos are about, because advertisers pay more to reach people thinking about money or software, and where your viewers live, because ads shown in the US cost far more than ads shown in India.

We start from typical RPM ranges for each niche with a US audience, scale them by your audience region, then multiply by your views. Shorts are paid differently and earn much less per view, so they use their own lower range.

How the calculator works

YouTube pays creators a share of ad revenue. What you earn per 1,000 views is called RPM (revenue per mille). It changes a lot with your niche, where your viewers live, video length and time of year. The calculator multiplies your daily views by a realistic RPM range for your niche and audience country, so you get an estimate per day, month and year instead of one misleading number.

Typical RPM ranges (estimates)

  • Finance, business, tech reviews: among the highest RPMs, especially with US/UK viewers.
  • Education and how-to: medium to high.
  • Gaming, vlogs, entertainment: lower RPM, but often more views.
  • Shorts: much lower RPM than long videos.
  • Indian audience: RPMs are usually lower than for US audiences, so a mixed audience earns more per view.

How to earn more per view

  • Make videos longer than 8 minutes so mid-roll ads can run.
  • Pick topics advertisers pay for: money, software, education.
  • Grow a global audience with English titles or subtitles (use Text to SRT).
  • Add income beyond ads: sponsors, affiliates, memberships.

Questions

How much does YouTube pay per 1,000 views?

Usually between $1 and $15 for long videos, depending on niche and audience country. Finance and tech channels with US viewers earn the most; music and entertainment channels with worldwide viewers earn the least.

How much do Shorts pay?

Far less than long videos: typically a few cents to around ten cents per 1,000 views, because Shorts revenue is shared across many creators and music licences.

How many views do I need to earn ₹10,000 a month?

Type your niche and region above and change the views until the monthly figure reaches ₹10,000. For an Indian audience in an average niche it's often a few hundred thousand views a month; for a US finance audience it can be far fewer.

When can I start earning?

Ad income requires the YouTube Partner Program, which currently needs 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 public watch hours in a year or 10 million Shorts views in 90 days.

Is this tool connected to my channel?

No. It's a calculator that runs in your browser. For your real numbers, open YouTube Studio, then Analytics, then Revenue.

How much does YouTube pay for 1,000 views in India?

It varies widely by niche and audience. Use the calculator with your niche and audience country for a realistic range; Shorts pay much less per view than long videos.

How much do I get for 1 lakh views?

Multiply 100 by your RPM in your currency. Enter 100,000 views in the calculator to see the range for your niche.
