How the timing works

We count the words you will actually say, skipping anything inside [square brackets] or (parentheses), since those are usually camera or sound notes. Then we divide by your speaking pace. A pause of about half a second is added between paragraphs, which matches how most narrators breathe between ideas.

Most YouTube narration runs at 150 to 170 words per minute. A 60-second Short is about 150 words. A 10-minute video is roughly 1,500 to 1,700 words.

How long will my script take?

Paste your script and pick a speaking pace. You get the total duration, a timestamp for every paragraph and how many words you need for your target length. Most people speak about 130–160 words per minute on camera; fast Shorts creators go 170+.

Quick reference

  • 30-second Short or Reel: about 70–85 words.
  • 60-second Short: about 140–160 words.
  • 8-minute YouTube video: about 1,100–1,250 words.
  • 10-minute video: about 1,400–1,600 words.

Tips

  • Time the hook separately: the first 3 seconds decide if people stay.
  • Use paragraph timestamps to plan B-roll and to make YouTube chapters.
  • Practise with the Teleprompter at the same pace.

Questions

How many words is a 10-minute YouTube video?

Roughly 1,500 words at a calm narration pace of 150 words per minute, or about 1,700 at a typical YouTube pace of 170.

How many words fit in a 60-second Short or Reel?

About 130 to 170 words, depending on how fast you speak. Aim for the lower end if you want room for on-screen moments.

Why are bracketed notes ignored?

Scripts usually contain directions like [B-roll] or (pause) that are never spoken. Leaving them out keeps the estimate accurate.

How do I find my own speaking pace?

Record yourself reading one paragraph, note the seconds, then enter words ÷ seconds × 60 as your words per minute.

Is my script uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything runs in your browser, and nothing you paste is sent to a server.

How many words is a 1-minute video?

About 130 to 160 words at a normal speaking pace.

Does it work for Hindi scripts?

Yes. It counts words in any language; Hindi speakers often speak slightly faster, so try the faster pace.
