    How it works

    The map is drawn on your device from open country outlines, so nothing is uploaded and there's no watermark. Flights curve along the shortest path over the globe, the way real planes fly; cars, trains and ships travel in straight lines on the map. The camera eases from one leg to the next, and every country you pass through can be highlighted. Turn on the globe intro to open with a spinning Earth that zooms into your first stop, and pick the Satellite style for real NASA imagery.

    Export in 720p, 1080p, 2K or 4K. Every frame is rendered one by one into an MP4, so nothing is dropped even at 4K; higher resolutions simply take longer. Keep this tab open while it renders.

    How to make an animated travel map

    1. Add your stops in order, cities or whole countries, for example Delhi → Dubai → Paris.
    2. Pick how you travel to each stop: plane, train, car, ship or walking.
    3. Choose one of ten styles, including explainer paper, night, vintage atlas, blueprint and real satellite imagery, with an optional 3D globe intro.
    4. Press Play to preview, then export MP4 in 720p, 1080p, 2K or 4K.

    Tips

    • Use 9:16 for Reels and Shorts, 16:9 for YouTube.
    • Keep it under 6 stops for a short video.
    • Satellite style looks best in 1080p or higher.

    Questions

    Is the video free to use on YouTube?

    Yes. The map outlines come from Natural Earth, which is in the public domain, the satellite imagery is NASA's Blue Marble (free to use), city locations come from GeoNames (CC BY 4.0), and there is no watermark. Use the videos in monetised videos, Reels and Shorts.

    Which places can I use?

    Every country, plus more than 24,000 towns and cities worldwide with at least 15,000 people. Type at least two letters to see suggestions. Add a country code to pick between places with the same name, like "Hyderabad, PK". For a smaller place, click the exact spot on the map and rename the stop.

    Why did my export come out as WebM?

    Some browsers can only record WebM. YouTube accepts WebM directly; for Instagram, open it in CapCut or any editor and export as MP4, or use Safari, which records MP4.

    Can I use it for history videos?

    Yes. Add the places in order, turn on country highlights and set a title such as "Napoleon's march, 1812" for a classic explainer look.

    Does the map show India's official borders?

    Yes. The map uses borders as shown from India's point of view.

    Is there a watermark?

    No. Exports are free and clean.
