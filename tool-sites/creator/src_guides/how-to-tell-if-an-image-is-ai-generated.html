AI images are now good enough to fool most people at a glance. No single check is perfect, but together these seven usually tell you what you are looking at.

1. Check the file for AI metadata

Many generators write into the image file. Stable Diffusion and ComfyUI save the prompt and settings, and tools such as ChatGPT, Adobe Firefly and others add Content Credentials (C2PA) or the IPTC tag "trained algorithmic media". The AI Image Detector reads these for you. If it finds them, the answer is close to certain.

2. Run an AI detector model

Detector models look at patterns in the pixels that people cannot see. The same tool runs one in your browser and shows a likelihood. Treat it as a strong hint, not proof: compression, filters and screenshots can fool any detector.

3. Look at hands, teeth and ears

Newer models are much better, but extra or merged fingers, uneven teeth and mismatched earrings still appear.

4. Read the text

Signs, labels and book covers in AI images often have warped or nonsense letters, especially in the background.

5. Check light and shadows

Do the shadows point the same way? Do reflections in glasses, windows and water match the scene?

6. Zoom into the background

Crowds, railings, wires and patterns often melt into each other or repeat strangely.

7. Find the source

A reverse image search shows where the image first appeared. A real news photo usually has a photographer, an agency and other photos from the same event.

Why detectors are sometimes wrong

Social media strips metadata, heavy editing can make real photos look synthetic, and brand-new generators may not have been seen by the model. That is why a good check shows its evidence instead of a bare "100% AI" label.

Questions

Can you detect Midjourney and ChatGPT images?

Original downloads often carry markers in the file. Screenshots and re-uploaded copies usually lose them, which leaves only visual checks and the detector model.
