How the calculation works

First we work out what one hour of your time is really worth after tax, counting the time you spend getting to work. Then we divide the price by that number.

yearly take-home = yearly pay × (1 − tax %)
hours your job takes = (hours per week + commute) × paid weeks
real hourly pay = yearly take-home ÷ hours your job takes
hours of work = price ÷ real hourly pay

For repeating costs we add up a full year of payments first. The "if invested" figure assumes you put the same money aside each month instead, earning the yearly return you choose, compounded monthly.

Questions

Why use take-home pay instead of my salary?

You can only spend what reaches your bank account. Using gross salary makes everything look cheaper than it really is.

Why does commuting change the answer?

Travel time is time your job takes even though you aren't paid for it. Counting it gives a more honest hourly figure. Set it to 0 if you work from home.

What should I enter for tax?

Look at a recent payslip: divide everything deducted (income tax, social security or pension contributions) by your gross pay. Most people land between 15% and 35%.

Is my information saved or sent anywhere?

No. The calculation runs in your browser. Your last inputs are remembered only on this device so you don't have to type them again.

What is a "workday" here?

Your weekly hours divided by five. If you work 40 hours a week, one workday is 8 hours.
