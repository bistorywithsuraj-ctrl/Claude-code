## 6. Research findings (cited, with dates)

> Labels: **[≤12 mo]** = published within the last 12 months (since Nov 2025). **[OLDER]** = more than 12 months old, so treat with care. A lot of "algorithm" content is vendor marketing; primary sources (Mosseri, Buffer/Sprout datasets) are weighted above blogs.

### Ranking signals
- **Sends per reach, likes per reach and watch time** are the three signals Mosseri has named. Sends matter "slightly more" than likes for reaching **non-followers**; likes matter more with followers. Mosseri's definition: "out of all the people who saw your reel or your photo, how many of them sent it to a friend in a DM?" Social Media Today, Jan 2025 **[OLDER]**, reaffirmed in 2026 round-ups (DesignRush 2026; blckalpaca "Instagram algorithm 2026" **[≤12 mo]**).
- The often-quoted claim that "sends are worth 3–5× likes" has **no primary source**. Don't plan around it.
- **Saves** aren't in Mosseri's named three. Third-party guides treat them as a secondary quality signal. Design for saves anyway: they are what lead to sends later.
- Instagram ranks Feed, Reels, Stories and Explore separately (Sprout Social, "Instagram algorithm", 2026 **[≤12 mo]**).
- **Carousel second chance:** Mosseri has said that if someone sees a carousel and doesn't swipe, Instagram will often show it to them again starting at **slide 2** (ccstartup, 21 Oct 2024 **[OLDER]**; repeated in 2026 guides). **So slide 2 is a second cover.** Ours (a frozen club, £140M → £2.5B) is strong: keep it.
- **Hashtags:** capped at 5 per post or Reel from 19 Dec 2025. Mosseri says they help classify a post but don't increase reach (Business Today, 19 Dec 2025; Social Media Today, Dec 2025 **[≤12 mo]**).

### Carousel benchmarks and length
- Carousels have the highest average engagement rate of any format, about **0.50–0.55% per follower** against about 0.48–0.52% for Reels (Socialinsider 2026 Organic Benchmarks, Q2–Q3 2026 **[≤12 mo]**; the figures on its pages are inconsistent).
- Buffer's 52M-post study: carousel median engagement by **reach 6.9% vs Reels 3.3%** (Buffer data via MEAN CEO summary, 2026 **[≤12 mo]**). It measures per reach, not per follower, so it isn't comparable with Socialinsider.
- **Length:** the limit is 20 slides (raised from 10 in 2024). Sources converge on **5–10 slides**: PostNext says swipe completion falls off sharply after about 10–12, and CreatorFlow 2026 recommends 8–10 (both blogs, directional only). **Recommendation: keep 8.** Go to 9–10 only when the extra slides are reference content such as ladders or tables.

### Best posting times
- Buffer (9.6M IG posts, Jan 2024 – Dec 2025, published **23 Sep 2026 [≤12 mo]**): Thu 09:00, Wed 12:00, Wed 18:00 in the audience's local time. Wednesday is the best day; Friday and Saturday are the weakest.
- Sprout Social (≈2B engagements, 307k profiles, updated **Mar 2026 [≤12 mo]**): Mon 2–4 pm, Tue 1–7 pm, Wed 12–9 pm, Thu 12–2 pm.
- India (WsCube Tech, TimeToPost, SchedPilot, 2026 **[≤12 mo]**, lower-quality methods): weekday evenings 7–9 pm IST and Wednesday are most often cited.

### How comparable explainer accounts build carousels
*Cited where a source exists. Where none does, the notes are my own reading of these brands' feeds; check them against their current posts before copying anything.*
- **The Economist:** about two-thirds of its 6.2M Instagram followers are 18–34, and it uses Instagram explicitly to reach a younger audience (Foreign Press / journalism.co.uk **[OLDER]**). Observed pattern: **one striking chart or number per frame**, a short headline, very little body text, and the chart *is* the save. → Our change 4.
- **Morning Brew / Marketing Brew:** Jack Appleby's "Future Social" for Morning Brew notes that **every swipe counts as engagement**, so story structure across slides feeds distribution (Marketing Brew, undated). Observed: a casual voice, meme-adjacent covers, a CTA to the newsletter.
- **Vox / Johnny Harris:** the map-based explainer grew out of Vox's Syria coverage around 2014–15 (Johnny Harris channel notes **[OLDER]**). Observed: **maps, clocks and real documents on paper**, which is exactly Greenback's look. → Use a world-clock map on slide 6.
- **Finshots and Zerodha Varsity (India):** no primary source found on their carousel strategy. Observed: Finshots runs one clean template, a question-led cover, about 5–8 dense text slides and a newsletter CTA. Varsity runs **numbered series** (Part 1/5) of simple illustrated concept slides that people save as notes. → Run "Money Machines" and "Case Files" as visible series, and number the cover (e.g. "CASE FILE #003").
- **Shared pattern across all six:** consistent templates and a recognisable series identity, one idea per frame, the data visual as the save, and the CTA pointing to a deeper product (newsletter or video). Greenback's YouTube episode is that deeper product.
